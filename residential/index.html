Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. San Clemente Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that many of them develop out of sight. Minerals build a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The date code on the label usually gives it away. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

A maintenance visit from San Clemente Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and confirms a safe water temperature. Small problems get caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the heater stays running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

The savings show up quickly. A flushed tank transfers heat with less effort, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old heater. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Needs change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks use far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes around San Clemente run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. San Clemente Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is climbing, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
