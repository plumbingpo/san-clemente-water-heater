Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

From the outside a tankless heater looks like a plain box; inside, it is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric models bring a different hazard, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
