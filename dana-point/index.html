Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and put down towels to slow the spread. Never ignore the smell of gas: get everyone out and call the gas utility from a safe spot.

Keeping Your Dana Point Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
